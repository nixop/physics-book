# 14.2. Radioactivity and the Interaction of Radiation with Matter

> [!abstract] In brief
> Alpha, beta, and gamma processes change a nucleus in different ways while conserving energy, momentum, charge, and applicable quantum numbers. One unstable nucleus decays at a random time, but a constant probability per unit time gives N(t) = N₀e^(−λt) for a large identical population.

## Core explanation

Activity A = λN counts decays per second but is not biological harm. Absorbed dose measures energy per mass in grays, equivalent dose weights radiation type in sieverts, and actual risk further depends on tissue, distribution, dose rate, and exposure conditions.

## Key ideas

- **Alpha, beta, and gamma decay.** — Alpha decay emits a ⁴He nucleus and lowers A by 4 and Z by 2. In beta-minus decay a neutron becomes a proton with an electron and antineutrino; beta-plus decay or electron capture lowers Z. A gamma transition removes excitation without changing A or Z.
- **The probabilistic law of decay and half-life.** — Half-life T₁/₂ = ln 2/λ characterizes an ensemble. A nucleus does not 'age' toward decay: for an exponential law, the conditional chance of decay in the next interval does not depend on its previous lifetime.
- **Activity, absorbed dose, and equivalent dose.** — 1 Bq is one decay per second; 1 Gy is 1 J/kg of absorbed energy. Equivalent dose H = ΣwᵣDᵣ uses radiation weighting factors and is measured in sieverts; these units are not interchangeable.
- **Shielding and biological effects.** — Shielding depends on energy and radiation type: alpha particles have short range but are dangerous internally; beta radiation can create bremsstrahlung in high-Z shielding; gamma rays are attenuated gradually, and neutrons need suitable hydrogen-rich and absorbing materials.

## Short example

- **Problem:** An iodine-131 sample has half-life 8.0 days and initial activity 80 MBq. What is its activity after 24 days if no new nuclei enter?
- **Answer:** After 24 days the activity is 10 MBq.
- **Check:** Every 8 days it halves: 80 to 40 to 20 to 10 MBq.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> One exponential describes an isolated population in one state with constant λ. Daughter chains, activation, and a changing source need coupled equations. Source activity cannot be converted to dose without geometry, spectrum, time, absorption, and biokinetics.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Half-life does not mean every nucleus is gone after two periods. On average one quarter remains, and an exponential never reaches exactly zero at finite time.

## Formulas

- [[../Formula Index#Law of Radioactive Decay|Law of Radioactive Decay]] — N(t) = N₀e^(−λt); A = λN; T₁/₂ = ln 2 / λ

## Self-check

1. Why does equal activity from two sources not guarantee equal equivalent dose to a person?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> List radiation type and energy, geometry, and route of intake.
> Each decay can deposit different energy with different biological effectiveness; distance, shielding, exposed tissue, and internal versus external location also matter.

2. A sample has activity 200 Bq and half-life 6 h. What is its activity after 18 h?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> 18 h is three half-lives.
> A = 200·(1/2)³ = 25 Bq.
