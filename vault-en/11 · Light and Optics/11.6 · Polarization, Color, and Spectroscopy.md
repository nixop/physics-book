# 11.6. Polarization, Color, and Spectroscopy

> [!abstract] In brief
> Polarization describes orientation of transverse electric field. An ideal linear polarizer selects one component, and an analyzer at angle theta transmits I = I0 cos^2(theta) for already linearly polarized input. Phase delay between orthogonal components creates elliptical and circular polarization.

## Core explanation

Color perception follows light spectrum, cone response, and visual processing, so distinct spectra can look identical. Atomic and molecular levels create characteristic absorption and emission lines. Their pattern indicates composition and conditions, while relative line shift reveals radial motion under an appropriate Doppler model.

## Key ideas

- **The polarization of light.** — Unpolarized light has randomly varying transverse-field direction, linear polarization fixes an axis, and circular polarization needs equal orthogonal components separated by phase pi/2.
- **Malus's law.** — Malus's law applies to an ideal analyzer and linearly polarized input; a first polarizer transmits half the mean intensity of ideal unpolarized light.
- **Color mixing and perception.** — Additive mixing of light differs from subtractive pigment mixing, and metamerism shows that one perceived color may come from different spectra.
- **Source spectra and spectral lines.** — Continuous, line, and band spectra reflect source and light-matter interaction; line width also carries information about temperature, collisions, and motion.
- **How light reveals the composition and motion of matter.** — Matching laboratory wavelengths to observed ones identifies elements and molecules, while systematic redshift or blueshift estimates line-of-sight motion.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A linearly polarized beam of intensity 100 W/m^2 reaches an ideal analyzer whose axis is 60 degrees from the polarization direction. Find output intensity.
- **Answer:** The analyzer transmits 25 W/m^2.
- **Check:** At 0 degrees all ideal input passes and at 90 degrees none passes; this result lies between.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Malus's law requires ideal linear polarizers and a known input state; Delta lambda/lambda = v_r/c requires |v_r| much less than c and line-of-sight motion, while strong fields, pressure, and cosmological expansion can shift or broaden lines differently.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Color cannot be assigned uniquely to one wavelength: white, purple, and many display colors are spectral mixtures.

## Self-check

1. Why do crossed ideal polarizers transmit almost no linearly polarized light?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Set theta = 90 degrees in Malus's law.
> Electric-field projection on the second axis is zero, so ideal intensity cos^2(90 degrees) is zero.

2. A 600.0 nm line is observed at 600.2 nm. Estimate small radial speed using c = 3.00e8 m/s.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> For a small shift, v_r/c is approximately Delta lambda/lambda.
> v_r is approximately 3.00e8 x 0.2/600 = 1.00e5 m/s; redshift denotes recession under this convention.
