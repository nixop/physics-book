# 11.5. Interference, Diffraction, and the Resolution Limit

> [!abstract] In brief
> Interference requires a stable phase difference over observation. In a double slit, indistinguishable coherent paths add by amplitude and far-field maxima obey d sin(theta_m) = m lambda. A broad source or spectrum averages different phases and reduces fringe visibility.

## Core explanation

Every finite aperture diffracts: a smaller transverse dimension produces a wider angular distribution. A many-slit grating gives narrow principal maxima, while a circular aperture gives an Airy pattern. The Rayleigh value theta approximately 1.22 lambda/D is a useful convention for distinguishing two equal points, not an absolute ban for every estimation method.

## Key ideas

- **Coherence.** — Temporal coherence limits allowed path difference through spectral width, while spatial coherence measures phase consistency across wavefront positions.
- **The double-slit experiment.** — Double-slit fringe locations follow path difference d sin(theta), while finite width of each slit multiplies fringes by a diffraction envelope.
- **Diffraction by an aperture and a grating.** — One slit gives a broad structure and many grating slits reinforce phase-matched directions; spectral resolving power grows with illuminated groove count and order.
- **The relationship between aperture size and the width of the diffraction pattern or diffraction spot.** — Reducing aperture increases angular width approximately as lambda/D even if a geometrical ray picture predicts a finer point.
- **The Rayleigh criterion.** — Under Rayleigh, one Airy maximum coincides with the other's first minimum; noise, contrast, prior model, and processing change practical distinguishability.

## Short example

- **Problem:** Two narrow slits are separated by d = 0.250 mm, wavelength is 500 nm, and a screen is L = 2.00 m away. Find adjacent-maxima spacing at small angles.
- **Answer:** Adjacent maxima are about 4.00 mm apart.
- **Check:** Larger d compresses fringes, while larger lambda or L expands them, as the formula predicts.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The formula d sin(theta_m) = m lambda requires monochromatic coherent slits and a far field or focal plane; 1.22 lambda/D applies to a uniformly illuminated circular aperture and a chosen resolution criterion.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Diffraction is not only bending at a geometrical edge: the whole complex wavefront across a finite aperture contributes to the far pattern.

## Self-check

1. Why do two independent ordinary lamps not produce stable double-source fringes?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Compare random relative-phase change time with exposure time.
> Their relative phase changes rapidly and randomly, so the cross-interference term averages nearly to zero.

2. Estimate Rayleigh angle for D = 0.050 m and lambda = 550 nm.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use theta = 1.22 lambda/D.
> theta is approximately 1.34e-5 rad, or about 2.8 arcseconds.
