# 1.6. Projections, Components, and Vector Products

> [!abstract] In brief
> A projection asks how much of a vector points along a selected axis. Components in an orthonormal basis reconstruct the entire vector and turn one vector equation into independent scalar equations along the axes.

## Core explanation

Vector products encode different geometry. The dot product measures alignment and appears in work, whereas the cross product measures oriented area and appears in torque and angular momentum.

## Key ideas

- **A projection as the 'shadow' of a vector on a chosen axis.** — The orthogonal projection aₓ = |a|cos θ is a signed number. It is the length of a shadow with a plus or minus sign determined by direction relative to the positive half-axis.
- **The sign of a projection and a zero projection.** — A vector perpendicular to an axis has zero projection although its magnitude is nonzero. A negative projection means opposite direction, not a negative length.
- **Moving from one axis to two- and three-dimensional coordinates.** — Two dimensions require two independent coordinates and three dimensions require three. Axes are a choice, but an orthonormal basis often simplifies lengths and products.
- **Reconstructing a vector from its components.** — A vector is the sum of components times basis vectors: a = aₓeₓ + aᵧeᵧ + a_z e_z. Under a basis change the numbers transform consistently while the geometrical vector does not change.
- **The dot product as a measure of alignment.** — a · b = |a||b|cos θ is positive for an acute angle, zero for perpendicular vectors, and negative for an obtuse angle. In an orthonormal basis it is the sum of componentwise products.
- **The cross product as an oriented area—an overview.** — |a × b| = |a||b|sin θ is the parallelogram area, with direction set by the right-hand rule. Interchanging the factors reverses the result.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A force F = (3, 4, 0) N acts through displacement s = (2, 0, 0) m; its point of application is r = (2, 0, 0) m from the origin. Find work and torque.
- **Answer:** The work is 6 J and the torque about the origin is (0, 0, 8) N·m.
- **Check:** The units need interpretation: work and torque magnitude both have dimension N·m, but torque is a rotational vector quantity and is not expressed in joules.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Simple component sums for length and dot product assume an orthonormal Euclidean basis; curvilinear or oblique coordinates require a metric and position-dependent basis vectors.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Do not confuse a component with a magnitude: a component may be negative, whereas vector magnitude is nonnegative by definition.

## Formulas

- [[../Formula Index#Dot Product of Vectors|Dot Product of Vectors]] — a · b = |a||b| cos θ = Σ_i a_i b_i

## Self-check

1. What does a · b = 0 imply for two nonzero Euclidean vectors?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use cos θ in the definition of the product.
> The vectors are perpendicular because nonzero magnitudes require cos θ = 0.

2. Calculate (2, −1, 3) · (4, 5, 0).

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Multiply corresponding components and add.
> 2×4 + (−1)×5 + 3×0 = 3. Its unit is the product of the units of the input quantities.
