# 1.5. Scalars and Vectors

> [!abstract] In brief
> A scalar is specified by a value with a unit and remains unchanged when coordinates are rotated, whereas a vector also carries a geometrical direction. Temperature and mass are scalars; displacement, velocity, and force are vectors.

## Core explanation

A vector can be drawn as an arrow and translated parallel to itself when it is free. Geometrical rules for addition and scalar multiplication do not depend on coordinates, although components change when axes rotate.

## Key ideas

- **Quantities that have only a value and quantities that also have a direction.** — The same positive speed does not say where an object moves. Velocity resolves this ambiguity as a vector, whereas scalar speed retains only its magnitude.
- **The magnitude and direction of a vector.** — Magnitude is a nonnegative scalar with the same unit as the vector. Direction may be given by an angle, a unit vector, or components relative to explicitly chosen axes.
- **Equality of free vectors regardless of their position.** — Two free vectors are equal when their magnitudes and directions agree. Their locations are irrelevant; for a bound vector such as a force on a rigid body, the point of application can affect rotation.
- **Vector addition and subtraction.** — A sum follows the triangle or parallelogram rule and represents sequential or combined action. The difference a − b is the sum of a and the reversed vector −b.
- **Multiplication of a vector by a scalar.** — Multiplication by a positive scalar changes magnitude, by a negative scalar also reverses direction, and multiplication by zero gives a zero vector with no defined direction.

## Short example

- **Problem:** An object moves by a = (3, 4) m and then by b = (−1, 2) m. Find the resultant vector, its magnitude, and its direction from +x.
- **Answer:** The resultant displacement is (2, 6) m, with magnitude about 6.32 m and direction 71.6° from +x.
- **Check:** Its magnitude does not exceed |a| + |b| = 5 + √5 ≈ 7.24 m, consistent with the triangle inequality.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Not every multicomponent quantity is an ordinary vector: stress is a tensor, and finite rotations in three dimensions do not add as free vectors.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Magnitudes may be added instead of vectors only when directions agree; in any other geometry this overestimates or underestimates the resultant.

## Self-check

1. Is work a scalar or a vector even though force and displacement are vectors?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Recall the type of result produced by a dot product.
> Work is a scalar: W = F · s. Its sign depends on the relative directions of force and displacement, but work itself has no spatial direction.

2. Find the sum of (5, −2) N and (−3, 7) N and its magnitude.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Add components, then use the Pythagorean theorem.
> The sum is (2, 5) N and its magnitude is √(2² + 5²) = √29 N ≈ 5.39 N.
