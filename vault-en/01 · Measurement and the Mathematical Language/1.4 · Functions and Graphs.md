# 1.4. Functions and Graphs

> [!abstract] In brief
> A function assigns an outcome to an independent variable while other conditions are fixed. A graph exposes sign, rate of change, extrema, and characteristic scales, but its meaning is determined by axis labels and the physical setup.

## Core explanation

Slope and area acquire units from their axes. The slope of position against time is velocity, and the area under velocity against time is displacement; the same geometric operations on different axes represent different quantities.

## Key ideas

- **Independent and dependent variables.** — An independent variable is selected or used as an argument, while a dependent variable is measured or calculated. This does not always imply causation: in observational data both may depend on a third quantity.
- **Direct, inverse, quadratic, and exponential relationships.** — For y ∝ x, y/x is constant; for y ∝ 1/x, xy is constant; and for y ∝ x², y/x² is constant. An exponential has a constant relative rather than absolute change over equal intervals.
- **Linear and logarithmic scales.** — A logarithmic axis maps equal ratios to equal distances. A straight line on a semilog plot indicates an exponential, while a straight line on a log-log plot indicates a power law for positive quantities.
- **The slope of a graph and the area under it.** — Local slope is a ratio of small coordinate changes with units of the vertical quantity per horizontal quantity. Signed area accumulates their product and counts regions below zero negatively.
- **Reading the physical meaning of a graph without substituting numbers.** — Before calculating, identify where a quantity rises, changes sign, or reaches an extremum. This qualitative prediction catches substitution errors and inappropriate scales.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A body's velocity is v(t) = 2 + 3t in SI units from 0 to 4 s. What do the slope and area under the graph represent?
- **Answer:** Acceleration is 3 m/s² and displacement over four seconds is 32 m along the positive axis.
- **Check:** The average velocity 8 m/s lies between the initial 2 and final 14 m/s, so 32 m has a sensible scale.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The apparent shape depends on axis range and scale; connecting sparse points does not establish behavior between them, and slope or area has physical meaning only for specifically defined quantities.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Visual steepness cannot be compared across graphs with different scales: the same numerical slope can look almost horizontal or almost vertical.

## Self-check

1. Why does a straight line on a plot of ln y against x indicate an exponential y(x)?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Write the straight-line equation for ln y and exponentiate it.
> If ln y = a + bx, then y = eᵃeᵇˣ. The slope b is a constant relative rate and eᵃ is the initial factor.

2. Velocity falls linearly from 12 m/s to zero over 6 s and remains nonnegative. Find the displacement.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> The region under v(t) is a triangle.
> The triangular area is ½ × 6 s × 12 m/s = 36 m.
