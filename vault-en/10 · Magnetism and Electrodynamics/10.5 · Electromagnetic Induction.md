# 10.5. Electromagnetic Induction

> [!abstract] In brief
> Magnetic flux Phi_B = integral B dot dS counts normal B through an oriented surface. Faraday's law connects its rate of change to circulation of induced E: emf = -dPhi_B/dt. Flux can change through B, area, orientation, or contour motion.

## Core explanation

The minus sign is Lenz's law: induced current produces a magnetic effect opposing flux change, not necessarily the original field. A mechanical generator therefore feels opposing torque under load. Closed eddy currents in bulk conductors heat material and create forces; lamination suppresses them, while braking and induction heating exploit them.

## Key ideas

- **Magnetic flux.** — Flux depends on selected surface orientation; reversing its normal reverses Phi_B and the consistently associated contour direction.
- **Faraday's law.** — Emf is field circulation around a closed path and exists without a metal wire; a conductor merely permits substantial current.
- **Lenz's law and conservation of energy.** — Opposition to change does not prevent flux growth: an external agent performs extra work that becomes electrical energy and heat.
- **Eddy currents.** — Eddy currents flow in bulk loops, so slots and insulated laminations limit loop area and reduce loss.
- **The electric generator.** — A generator changes coil flux mechanically and thereby converts mechanical work into electrical energy.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A coil with N = 200 and turn area 5.0e-3 m^2 rotates at 50 Hz in uniform B = 0.40 T. Its rotation axis is perpendicular to B. Find emf amplitude.
- **Answer:** Emf amplitude is approximately 126 V.
- **Check:** Doubling rotation frequency would double flux-change rate and emf amplitude.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The simple form -N dPhi/dt assumes equal flux through N turns and a defined contour; at high frequency, distributed fields, skin effect, and parasitic capacitance require a full electromagnetic model.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Induction depends on flux change, not on a large constant flux: a stationary coil in a constant uniform field can have zero emf.

## Self-check

1. Which way does current flow if outward magnetic flux through the page increases?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Induced field must oppose the increase.
> The coil creates field into the page, so the right-hand rule gives clockwise current.

2. One-turn flux falls uniformly from 8 to 2 mWb in 0.030 s. Find average emf magnitude.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use |Delta Phi|/Delta t.
> |emf| = 6.0e-3/0.030 = 0.20 V.
