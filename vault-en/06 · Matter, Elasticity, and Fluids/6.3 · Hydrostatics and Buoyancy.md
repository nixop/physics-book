# 6.3. Hydrostatics and Buoyancy

> [!abstract] In brief
> A fluid at rest has no shear stress, and pressure acts normal to every surface. Balancing a small volume in a uniform gravitational field gives dp/dz = -rho g: pressure increases downward. A pressure difference, rather than atmospheric pressure by itself, creates a net hydrostatic force.

## Core explanation

Integrating pressure over an immersed surface produces the buoyant force, equal to the weight of displaced fluid. A floating body settles at an immersed volume for which buoyancy equals its weight. Orientational stability also depends on the lines of action of weight and buoyancy, not only on equality of their magnitudes.

## Key ideas

- **How pressure varies with depth.** — For a liquid of constant density, p = p0 + rho g h; points at equal height in one connected stationary liquid have equal pressure.
- **Pascal's principle and the hydraulic press.** — An externally applied pressure increment is transmitted through an enclosed liquid, so an ideal hydraulic press changes force in the area ratio while conserving work.
- **Buoyant force.** — Buoyancy points opposite to effective gravity and has magnitude rho_fluid g V_displaced; it arises because lower parts of the surface experience greater pressure.
- **Conditions for flotation and stability.** — Floating equilibrium requires F_b = mg, and a small tilt must produce a restoring torque; the metacentre relative to the centre of mass supplies a small-angle stability criterion.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A uniform block of volume 0.020 m^3 and mass 12 kg floats freely in water of density 1000 kg/m^3. Find the displaced volume and the fraction below water.
- **Answer:** The block displaces 0.012 m^3 of water and has 60% of its volume submerged.
- **Check:** Its mean density is 600 kg/m^3, below that of water, so partial flotation is possible; g cancels as expected.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Hydrostatic relations require a fluid at rest in the chosen frame and a known effective gravity field; accelerated flow, appreciable compressibility, or dominant capillary forces require more than p = p0 + rho gh.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Buoyancy need not equal an object's weight: that equality holds only for vertical equilibrium of a freely floating object with no additional vertical forces.

## Self-check

1. Why is pressure at a vessel bottom not determined uniquely by the total mass of liquid?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Compare p = p0 + rho gh for differently shaped vessels having the same depth.
> Local pressure depends on depth, density, and surface pressure; vessel walls may carry part of the liquid weight, and bottom force also depends on bottom area.

2. What is the gauge pressure 5.0 m below the surface of fresh water? Take g = 9.81 m/s^2.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use Delta p = rho gh with rho = 1000 kg/m^3.
> Delta p = 4.905e4 Pa, approximately 49 kPa.
