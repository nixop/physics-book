# 13. Quantum Physics, Atomic Physics, and Materials Physics

> [!info] Section
> 8 cards · about 34 planned pages · level: advanced

## Cards

- [[13.1 · The Crisis of Classical Physics and the Photon|13.1. The Crisis of Classical Physics and the Photon]] — Classical electrodynamics correctly describes light propagation, but combined with classical statistics it gives the wrong high-frequency thermal spectrum.
- [[13.2 · Matter Waves and the Double-Slit Experiment|13.2. Matter Waves and the Double-Slit Experiment]] — The de Broglie hypothesis assigns a free particle wavelength λ = h/p.
- [[13.3 · Quantum State and Measurement|13.3. Quantum State and Measurement]] — A quantum state encodes the most complete predictions available within the theory for a specified preparation.
- [[13.4 · Uncertainty and the Schrödinger Equation|13.4. Uncertainty and the Schrödinger Equation]] — A localized state is a wave packet built from components with different wave numbers, so compressing the packet requires a broader momentum range.
- [[13.5 · Spin, Entanglement, and Decoherence|13.5. Spin, Entanglement, and Decoherence]] — Spin is an intrinsic quantum degree of freedom with discrete projection results; it cannot literally be modeled as a rotating charged ball.
- [[13.6 · Atoms, Molecules, and Spectra|13.6. Atoms, Molecules, and Spectra]] — Rutherford scattering showed that positive charge and nearly all atomic mass occupy a tiny nucleus.
- [[13.7 · Solid-State Physics and Semiconductors|13.7. Solid-State Physics and Semiconductors]] — The lattice's periodic potential and the Pauli principle turn discrete atomic levels from an enormous number of atoms into allowed energy bands.
- [[13.8 · Quantum Technologies|13.8. Quantum Technologies]] — Quantum technology extends far beyond quantum computers.
