# 4.7. Rotational Kinematics and Dynamics

> [!abstract] In brief
> Rotation about a fixed axis is described by angle, angular velocity, and angular acceleration just as translation uses position, velocity, and acceleration. The analogue of mass is moment of inertia I = ∫r²dm, which weights mass by distance from the axis.

## Core explanation

Rotational energy is Iω²/2. In rolling without slipping, the center of mass translates, the body rotates about it, and v_cm = ωR links the two motions.

## Key ideas

- **Angle, angular velocity, and angular acceleration.** — Angular variables satisfy α = dω/dt and ω = dφ/dt. Constant-α equations parallel constant-acceleration kinematics when angle is in radians.
- **Moment of inertia and mass distribution.** — Equal masses can have different I: moving mass farther from the axis increases its contribution as r². The parallel-axis theorem gives I = I_cm + Md².
- **Rotational kinetic energy.** — Rotational energy depends on chosen axis and angular velocity. General rigid-body motion can be split as K = Mv_cm²/2 + I_cmω²/2.
- **Rolling without slipping.** — Without slipping, the contact point is instantaneously at rest relative to the surface and v_cm = ωR. Static friction may enforce this condition without doing work at a stationary contact point.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A solid cylinder starts from rest and rolls without slipping through height 1.20 m. Find center-of-mass speed at the bottom for I_cm = MR²/2 and g = 9.81 m/s².
- **Answer:** Cylinder-center speed is about 3.96 m/s in the ideal no-loss, no-slip model.
- **Check:** It is below √(2gh) ≈ 4.85 m/s for a nonrotating sliding point because some energy is rotational.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> A rigid-body model needs negligible deformation and v_cm = ωR needs no slip; moment of inertia belongs to a specified axis and is not one universal number for a body.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Do not assign all Mgh to Mv²/2 for a rolling body; rotational kinetic energy is generally nonzero.

## Self-check

1. Two rings of equal mass rotate at equal ω, but one has a larger radius. Which has more energy?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> For a thin ring I = MR².
> The larger ring, because K = MR²ω²/2 grows as R².

2. A wheel of radius 0.30 m rolls without slipping at ω = 10 rad/s. Find center speed.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use v_cm = ωR.
> v_cm = 10×0.30 = 3.0 m/s.
