# 4.5. Momentum and Impulse

> [!abstract] In brief
> Momentum p = mv combines mass with vector velocity. Newton's law gives momentum change as net impulse J = ∫Fdt, so area under a force-time graph matters rather than peak force alone.

## Core explanation

A system's total momentum changes through external impulse. Internal forces redistribute momentum among parts, but when external impulse is zero the vector sum is conserved and recoil follows.

## Key ideas

- **The momentum of a body.** — Momentum points with velocity and has unit kg·m/s. At equal speed, a more massive body requires a larger impulse to stop.
- **Impulse as a change in momentum.** — J = Δp is exact for resultant force. Mean force over an interval is Fmean = Δp/Δt, but it does not reproduce the shape of a short force peak.
- **Conservation of momentum.** — If external impulse is negligible, Pbefore = Pafter. This vector law applies component by component; kinetic energy may still change.
- **Recoil and rocket propulsion.** — In recoil, two parts receive opposite momentum changes. Rocket motion is a continuous transfer of momentum in expelled mass.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A 0.15 kg ball travels at 20 m/s along +x and rebounds at 15 m/s along −x. Contact lasts 0.010 s. Find momentum change and mean resultant force.
- **Answer:** Momentum changes by −5.25 kg·m/s and mean resultant force is −525 N, opposite the initial motion.
- **Check:** Reversal requires changing momentum by the sum of magnitudes 3.00 + 2.25 = 5.25 kg·m/s.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The expression p = mv is nonrelativistic, and momentum conservation requires all momentum carriers plus a check of external impulse; electromagnetic field momentum may be part of the system.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Do not subtract speed magnitudes during a rebound: final velocity has the opposite sign, making momentum change larger than for a simple stop.

## Self-check

1. Why does an airbag reduce mean force on a passenger for the same velocity change?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Relate fixed Δp to interaction duration.
> It lengthens stopping time, so |Fmean| = |Δp|/Δt decreases. Peak force also depends on the time-profile shape.

2. A 4 kg cart at rest ejects a 1 kg load at +6 m/s relative to the ground. Find the remaining 3 kg cart velocity when external impulse is zero.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Initial total momentum is zero.
> 1×6 + 3v = 0, so v = −2 m/s.
