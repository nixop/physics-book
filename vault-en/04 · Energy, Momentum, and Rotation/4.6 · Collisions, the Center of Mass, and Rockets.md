# 4.6. Collisions, the Center of Mass, and Rockets

> [!abstract] In brief
> During a short collision, external impulse is often negligible, so total system momentum is conserved. Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision; in an inelastic collision some becomes internal energy, with sticking giving the largest loss for fixed initial momenta.

## Core explanation

The center of mass moves as if total mass were concentrated there and acted on by net external force. A rocket changes mass and transfers momentum to exhaust, so its correct balance includes mass flux through the boundary.

## Key ideas

- **Elastic and inelastic collisions.** — An elastic collision conserves momentum and total kinetic energy; an inelastic collision conserves isolated-system momentum but not kinetic energy.
- **Momentum is conserved in an isolated system; kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision.** — P conservation follows from zero external impulse. Internal deformation can increase internal energy, so Kbefore = Kafter needs a separate physical condition.
- **The center of mass and motion of the system as a whole.** — Center-of-mass position is R = Σmᵢrᵢ/M and M d²R/dt² = Fext for a fixed particle set. Internal motion does not alter this law.
- **A rocket is an open system: its momentum balance includes the flux of expelled mass.** — If the system is the remaining rocket alone, combustion products cross its boundary and carry momentum. Changing mass is not itself an external force.
- **The fixed-composition formula `F = d(mv)/dt` cannot be applied to a rocket without an additional momentum-flux term.** — Differentiating mv for changing composition without a flux term mixes momentum of different particle sets. Apply a balance to rocket plus a small fuel parcel over the same interval.
- **The simplest Tsiolkovsky rocket equation.** — For an ideal rocket with no external force and constant effective exhaust speed u_e, Δv = u_e ln(m₀/m_f). Exhaust speed is measured relative to the rocket.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A 2.0 kg body moving at 3.0 m/s sticks to a stationary 1.0 kg body. Find their common speed and kinetic-energy change.
- **Answer:** The joined bodies move at 2.0 m/s and kinetic energy decreases by 3 J.
- **Check:** Final momentum 3×2 = 6 kg·m/s equals initial momentum although K is not conserved.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The elementary Tsiolkovsky equation neglects gravity and drag during the burn, assumes constant exhaust speed, and compares initial and final masses; real maneuvers integrate external forces and variable thrust.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Do not impose both momentum and kinetic-energy conservation on every collision; the second equality holds only for an elastic collision.

## Self-check

1. How does center-of-mass velocity change during an internal collision when external force is zero?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Apply the center-of-mass law to the full system.
> It does not change; center-of-mass velocity remains constant while individual velocities can change abruptly.

2. An ideal rocket has u_e = 3000 m/s and mass ratio m₀/m_f = e. Find ideal velocity increment.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> ln e = 1.
> Δv = u_e ln(m₀/m_f) = 3000 m/s, excluding gravity and aerodynamic losses.
