# 4.2. Kinetic Energy and the Work–Energy Theorem

> [!abstract] In brief
> In nonrelativistic mechanics, particle kinetic energy is K = mv²/2. The work-energy theorem states that work by the resultant force along a path equals the change in K, regardless of how individual forces share that work.

## Core explanation

The theorem is especially useful when force depends on position and time is not needed. It connects two states, but work signs and kinetic-energy values belong to the selected inertial frame.

## Key ideas

- **The energy of motion.** — K is a scalar and grows with speed squared: doubling speed at fixed mass multiplies energy by four. Its zero depends on the frame in which the body rests.
- **The relationship between net work and the change in kinetic energy.** — Newton's law gives dK = Fres·dr. Positive net work increases speed, negative net work decreases it, and zero net work preserves speed even if direction changes.
- **Braking distance.** — With an approximately constant braking force, stopping distance is proportional to v₀². A modest percentage increase in speed therefore causes a larger increase in minimum braking distance.
- **Work in different frames of reference.** — Velocity, point-of-application displacement, and work change between inertial frames. Wres = ΔK remains true when every quantity is evaluated in the same frame.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A 1000 kg car travels at 20 m/s. A constant 5000 N resultant braking force opposes motion. Find stopping distance.
- **Answer:** Ideal braking distance is 40 m; driver reaction distance and variable grip are excluded.
- **Check:** Doubling initial speed would quadruple energy and distance under the same force.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> K = mv²/2 requires constant mass and v ≪ c; an extended rotating body has additional rotational energy, while relativistic motion uses K = (γ−1)mc².

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Work by one selected force generally does not equal kinetic-energy change; the theorem uses net work by all forces on the body.

## Self-check

1. Can a nonzero force act continuously without changing kinetic energy?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Consider a force perpendicular to velocity.
> Yes. In uniform circular motion a radial force redirects velocity but F·v = 0 and K stays constant.

2. A 2.0 kg body speeds up from 3.0 to 7.0 m/s. Find net work.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use ΔK = m(v₂²−v₁²)/2.
> Wres = ½×2.0×(49−9) = 40 J.
