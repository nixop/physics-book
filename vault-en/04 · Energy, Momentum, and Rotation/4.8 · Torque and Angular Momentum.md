# 4.8. Torque and Angular Momentum

> [!abstract] In brief
> Torque τ = r×F measures an external force's ability to alter rotation about a chosen origin. External torque is the time derivative of total system angular momentum, so L is conserved when that torque vanishes.

## Core explanation

For a particle L = r×p and system contributions are summed. For a rigid body about a fixed principal axis, L = Iω often applies, but in general L and ω need not be parallel, which underlies precession.

## Key ideas

- **Lever arm and torque.** — Torque magnitude is force times perpendicular distance to its line of action. A force whose line passes through the origin has zero torque about that origin.
- **Static equilibrium.** — Static rigid-body equilibrium requires both ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0. Zero net force with nonzero torque still permits angular acceleration.
- **For a point particle, `L = r × p`; for a system, `L = Σᵢ rᵢ × pᵢ` about the chosen origin.** — A particle's orbital angular momentum depends on r and the selected origin. Every contribution to a system sum must use the same origin and inertial frame.
- **The total angular momentum of a system is conserved when the external torque vanishes.** — If total external torque about the selected origin is zero, total L is conserved. Internal forces can redistribute it among parts and kinds of rotation.
- **Gyroscopic stability and precession.** — A rapidly spinning rotor with large L changes direction gradually under torque, producing precession. This realizes rather than violates dL/dt = τ.
- **Symmetries as the origin of conservation laws—an overview.** — The link between continuous rotational symmetry and angular-momentum conservation is formalized by Noether's theorem; it requires invariant dynamics, not merely a round object.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A skater rotates under negligible external torque with I₁ = 4.0 kg·m² and ω₁ = 2.0 rad/s, then pulls in their arms to I₂ = 2.0 kg·m². Find new angular speed and energy change.
- **Answer:** Angular speed doubles to 4.0 rad/s and rotational energy rises by 8 J through muscular work.
- **Check:** Angular momentum remains 8.0 kg·m²/s in both states; energy need not be separately conserved.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The simple L = Iω relation applies to rotation about a principal axis; torque and angular momentum must use one origin fixed in an inertial frame unless additional transport terms are included.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Angular-momentum conservation does not mean constant angular velocity: changing mass distribution changes I and is compensated by ω.

## Self-check

1. Why is a door handle placed far from its hinges?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Compare force lever arms for the same effort.
> A larger perpendicular lever arm gives greater torque τ = r⊥F, so less force produces the same rotational effect.

2. A particle has r = (2, 0, 0) m and p = (0, 3, 0) kg·m/s. Find L about the origin.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Calculate r×p and apply the right-hand rule.
> L = (0, 0, 6) kg·m²/s, directed along +z.
