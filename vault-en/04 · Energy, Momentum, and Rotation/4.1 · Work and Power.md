# 4.1. Work and Power

> [!abstract] In brief
> Mechanical work measures energy transfer by a force along the actual displacement. For a constant force W = F·Δr, so only the longitudinal component contributes; a perpendicular force may redirect motion without doing work.

## Core explanation

For a variable force, work is a line integral along the path. Power describes the rate of energy transfer: average power is W/Δt and instantaneous power is P = F·v for a force on a particle.

## Key ideas

- **Work done by a constant or variable force.** — For a constant force, work is FΔr cos θ. It is positive for an acute angle, negative when the force opposes displacement, and zero when they are perpendicular.
- **The role of the component of force along the displacement.** — Projection along an elementary displacement determines dW = F·dr. A large transverse force, such as an ideal centripetal force, does not change kinetic energy.
- **Work as the area under a force graph.** — On a graph of longitudinal force Fₓ against x, signed area is ∫Fₓdx. Regions below the axis contribute negatively, and the unit N·m is one joule.
- **Power and the rate of energy transfer.** — Power states how rapidly the same work is performed. In rotation, P = τ·ω is the analogue of P = F·v.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A force along an axis is Fₓ = 2x N with x in meters. A body moves from x = 0 to x = 3 m in 2 s. Find work and average power.
- **Answer:** The force does 9 J of work and average power over the interval is 4.5 W.
- **Check:** The triangular area under the graph, with base 3 m and height 6 N, is ½×3×6 = 9 J.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Work depends on the reference frame and on the path of the point of application. For a constant force vector, W = F·Δr depends only on the total displacement regardless of path shape; a varying force requires the integral ∫F·dr.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Work is not the product of arbitrary magnitudes F and s but a dot product; normal force does no work during horizontal displacement even when it is large.

## Formulas

- [[../Formula Index#Work–Energy Theorem|Work–Energy Theorem]] — W_net = ∫ F_net · dr = ΔK; K = mv²/2

## Self-check

1. Does ideal string tension do work on a body in uniform circular motion?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Compare tension direction with instantaneous velocity.
> No. Tension is radial and velocity tangential, so F·v = 0 and kinetic energy remains constant.

2. A hoist steadily raises a load with 500 N through 2.0 m in 4.0 s. Find work and average power.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Force and displacement are aligned.
> W = 500×2.0 = 1000 J and P̄ = 1000/4.0 = 250 W.
