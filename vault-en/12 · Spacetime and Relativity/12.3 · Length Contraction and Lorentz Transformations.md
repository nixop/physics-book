# 12.3. Length Contraction and Lorentz Transformations

> [!abstract] In brief
> Length is the distance between endpoint positions measured simultaneously in the frame where the answer is required. Because simultaneity depends on the frame, the two laboratory endpoint events are not a simultaneous pair in the rod's rest frame.

## Core explanation

Lorentz transformations change x and t consistently while preserving the spacetime interval. A rod of proper length L₀ moving parallel to itself has laboratory length L = L₀/γ. The same transformation structure gives the relativistic velocity-addition law that preserves the limiting speed c.

## Key ideas

- **Measuring the length of a moving body.** — The front and rear positions must be recorded at one laboratory time. An uncorrected photograph is not this direct measurement because light from different points reaches the camera at different times.
- **Length contraction.** — Only the component parallel to relative motion contracts; transverse dimensions do not acquire a factor 1/γ. L₀ is measured in the object's rest frame and is therefore called proper length.
- **The Lorentz factor.** — The factor γ = 1/√(1 - v²/c²) is at least one, grows rapidly as v approaches c, and differs from one only by about v²/(2c²) at low speed.
- **Transformations of coordinates and time.** — For S' moving at +v along x relative to S, a clear sign convention is x' = γ(x - vt), t' = γ(t - vx/c²). Reversing v reverses the corresponding signs.
- **Relativistic velocity addition.** — If an object has speed u in S, then S' measures u' = (u - v)/(1 - uv/c²). The denominator comes from transforming time and prevents subluminal velocities from combining into a superluminal one.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A rod's proper length is 100 m. It moves along its axis at 0.80c relative to a laboratory. What length do laboratory sensors triggered simultaneously obtain?
- **Answer:** The longitudinal laboratory length is 60 m; in the rod frame its length remains 100 m.
- **Check:** The result is below the proper length and would approach 100 m as the speed approaches zero.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The equation L = L₀/γ concerns endpoint separation in an inertial frame and assumes a rod at rest as a whole in another inertial frame. An extended accelerating body requires a separate treatment of rigidity, and its visual appearance is also altered by light-travel time.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Length contraction is not mechanical compression in the object's own frame. It is the difference between spatial slices through the same extended world tube made by different observers.

## Self-check

1. Why can one not measure a moving train's laboratory length by marking the locomotive position and then, one second later, the last carriage position?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Recall the operational definition of length in a chosen frame.
> That coordinate difference mixes the train's displacement with its size. Both endpoint positions must be recorded at the same laboratory time.

2. A particle moves at 0.90c in a laboratory, while an observer follows it at 0.50c. What particle speed does the observer measure?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use u' = (u - v)/(1 - uv/c²).
> u' = (0.90 - 0.50)c/(1 - 0.90·0.50) = 0.40c/0.55 ≈ 0.727c.
