# 12.4. Spacetime Diagrams and Causality

> [!abstract] In brief
> A Minkowski diagram represents an event by coordinates x and ct and an object's history by a worldline. With equal axis scales, light travels at 45 degrees and forms the light-cone boundary; a massive object's worldline must remain inside that cone.

## Core explanation

The interval s² = c²Δt² - Δx² is the same in every inertial frame for the chosen sign convention. A positive interval permits causal influence and a frame where both events occur at one place; zero describes light; a negative interval means no subluminal signal can connect the events.

## Key ideas

- **Worldlines.** — A worldline's slope encodes speed: with ct vertical, |dx/d(ct)| equals |v|/c. A vertical line is rest in that frame, while a bend indicates changing velocity.
- **Light cones.** — Future and past light cones contain events respectively reachable from an event or able to influence it. Their boundaries are the rays x = ±ct.
- **The spacetime interval.** — Interval invariance unifies time dilation, length contraction, and causality. For a timelike pair, proper time is Δτ = √(Δt² - Δx²/c²).
- **Causally connected and causally disconnected events.** — The order of timelike and lightlike events is preserved for all inertial observers. A spacelike pair may reverse order precisely because neither event can cause the other.
- **The twin paradox and other apparent contradictions.** — The twins compare proper times along different worldlines between common meetings. The traveler changes inertial segments at turnaround, so the situations are not symmetric; the age difference is determined by the complete paths, not by a momentary 'feeling' of speed.

## Short example

- **Problem:** Two events are 5.0 μs apart in laboratory time and 1.20 km apart in position. Determine the interval type and the proper time between them.
- **Answer:** The interval is timelike, causal influence is possible, and a clock moving uniformly from the first event to the second records about 3.0 μs.
- **Check:** The required average speed is 1.20 km/5.0 μs = 0.80c, below c and consistent with a timelike interval.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> A flat x-ct diagram suppresses two spatial coordinates and represents special relativity. In curved spacetime light cones are defined locally and intervals come from the metric; long lines on the simple plot cannot be interpreted as globally Euclidean.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> A line's visual angle has physical meaning only when x and ct use equal scales. Stretching an axis can make a subluminal worldline look 'faster than light' even though its coordinates are valid.

## Self-check

1. What does negative s² mean when s² = c²Δt² - Δx²?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Compare Δx with the maximum light-travel distance c|Δt|.
> The separation is spacelike: |Δx| > c|Δt|, no causal signal can connect the events, and inertial frames may disagree about their time order.

2. Events are separated by 600 m and 1.0 μs. Can the first cause the second?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Find Δx/Δt or the distance light covers in that time.
> No. The required speed is 6.0·10⁸ m/s ≈ 2c, so the separation is spacelike.
