# 12.5. Relativistic Energy and Momentum

> [!abstract] In brief
> For a particle of invariant mass m, relativistic momentum is p = γmv and total energy is E = γmc². At low speed, expansion gives E ≈ mc² + mv²/2: Newtonian kinetic energy appears as the addition to a much larger rest energy.

## Core explanation

Energy and momentum obey the invariant E² - p²c² = m²c⁴. Collisions and decays therefore conserve total E and vector p, not a separate sum of rest masses. The invariant mass of an interacting multiparticle system can include internal and kinetic energy of its constituents.

## Key ideas

- **Relativistic momentum at high speeds.** — The factor γ makes momentum nonlinear in speed and prevents any finite force from accelerating a massive particle to c: the required momentum and energy diverge as v approaches c.
- **Rest energy is given by `E₀ = mc²`.** — E₀ = mc² refers to a particle or system in its center-of-momentum frame. It does not say that the total energy of a moving particle is always only mc².
- **The total energy of a free particle obeys `E² = p²c² + m²c⁴`.** — The relation E² = p²c² + m²c⁴ also works for m = 0, giving E = pc for a photon in vacuum.
- **Total energy is conserved in transformations; rest energy can become kinetic energy and vice versa.** — In a reaction, rest energy of the initial system may become product kinetic energy, or supplied energy may increase a composite system's invariant mass. The total four-dimensional quantity remains conserved.
- **Four-momentum as an optional extension.** — Four-momentum P = (E/c, p) transforms as one geometric object. Its square P² = m²c² is invariant and is especially useful for thresholds and decays.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A proton has rest energy 938 MeV and momentum 750 MeV/c. Find its total and kinetic energies.
- **Answer:** The total energy is about 1.20 GeV and the kinetic energy is 263 MeV.
- **Check:** Total energy exceeds both pc and rest energy; substitution back gives E² - p²c² ≈ (938 MeV)².

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The formulas E = γmc² and p = γmv describe a free point particle with constant invariant mass. A bound system requires all internal energy and its total four-momentum; in curved spacetime, global energy conservation requires an appropriate symmetry of the geometry.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Avoid 'relativistic mass' unless a context specifically defines it. It is clearer to keep one invariant mass m and separately state the frame-dependent energy E and momentum p.

## Self-check

1. Can one free electron completely absorb one photon in empty space?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Try to conserve energy and momentum together and compare the invariant masses of the initial and final states.
> No. The electron-plus-photon system has invariant mass greater than the electron mass, whereas a lone final electron must retain its mass. Another object is needed to take recoil.

2. A particle moves at 0.80c. What fraction of its rest energy is its kinetic energy?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> K/E₀ = γ - 1.
> γ = 5/3, so K/E₀ = 2/3 ≈ 0.667.
