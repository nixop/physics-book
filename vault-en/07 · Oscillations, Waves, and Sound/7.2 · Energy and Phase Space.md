# 7.2. Energy and Phase Space

> [!abstract] In brief
> One coordinate is insufficient for a one-dimensional oscillator: at the same position the body may move right or left. The pair (x,v) specifies its instantaneous state and fixes subsequent motion when the law is known. A plot of v against x is a phase trajectory.

## Core explanation

For an ideal harmonic oscillator, $E = mv^2/2 + kx^2/2$ is constant, so the phase trajectory is a closed ellipse. Loss contracts the trajectory toward equilibrium, driving can sustain a limit cycle, and different regions of phase space reveal qualitatively distinct regimes without watching a long animation.

## Key ideas

- **The exchange between kinetic and potential energy.** — At turning points, speed and kinetic energy vanish while potential energy is maximal; at the equilibrium crossing, speed and kinetic energy are maximal.
- **The state of a system as a position–velocity pair.** — The sign of velocity distinguishes two states with the same coordinate, so the pair (x,v) removes the ambiguity of a position snapshot.
- **A phase-space trajectory.** — Each point on the phase curve is a state and time sets its direction; two trajectories cannot cross for a deterministic autonomous equation with a unique solution.
- **What a phase portrait reveals better than a conventional animation.** — A phase portrait immediately displays equilibria, stability, closed oscillation, decay, and regions of different motion even when time traces look complicated.

## Short example

- **Problem:** An oscillator has amplitude $A = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\omega_{0} = 5.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. What is its speed magnitude at $x = 0.048\,\mathrm{m}$? Neglect loss.
- **Answer:** The speed magnitude is 0.32 m/s; its sign depends on direction around the phase trajectory.
- **Check:** At $x = 0$ the speed would be $A \omega_{0} = 0.40\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so this result is correctly below the maximum.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The two-dimensional plane (x,v) is complete only for one coordinate governed by a second-order equation; a driven system also needs driving phase, and a multidimensional system needs additional coordinates and momenta.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> A phase trajectory is not the body's path in ordinary space: its axes carry different quantities, such as metres and metres per second.

## Self-check

1. How can a phase portrait distinguish ideal oscillation from damped oscillation?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Track whether the closed curve keeps the same size with time.
> An ideal oscillator follows a closed curve of constant size, while a damped oscillator spirals toward stable equilibrium.

2. For $A = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\omega_{0} = 4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, find the maximum speed.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Maximum speed occurs at $x = 0$.
> $v_{\mathrm{max}} = A \omega_{0} = 0.40\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
