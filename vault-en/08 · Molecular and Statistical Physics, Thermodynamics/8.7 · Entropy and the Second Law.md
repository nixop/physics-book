# 8.7. Entropy and the Second Law

> [!abstract] In brief
> A macrostate is fixed by a few measured variables, whereas a microstate specifies all microscopic degrees of freedom. Equilibrium is overwhelmingly likely because it usually corresponds to most accessible microstates. For Omega equiprobable states, S = k_B ln(Omega); for discrete probabilities, S_G = -k_B sum p_i ln(p_i).

## Core explanation

Thermodynamic entropy change between equilibrium states can be computed along an auxiliary reversible path: Delta S = integral(delta Q_rev/T). The second law requires nondecrease of total entropy for an isolated system. Entropy of a selected subsystem can fall if entropy transfer to the surroundings and entropy production are included in the full balance.

## Key ideas

- **Microstates and macrostates.** — Many different particle positions and momenta give the same p, V, and T; entropy connects macroscopic description to the multiplicity of these realizations.
- **The probabilistic nature of equilibrium.** — A fluctuation away from equilibrium is possible, but in a macroscopic system the fraction of corresponding microstates is ordinarily extraordinarily small.
- **For `Ω` equiprobable microstates, entropy is `S = k_B ln Ω`; for a discrete classical ensemble, Gibbs entropy is `S_G = −k_B Σᵢ pᵢ ln pᵢ`.** — The logarithm makes entropy additive for independent systems because joint microstate counts multiply while their logarithms add.
- **The second law applies to the total entropy of an isolated system.** — For an isolated system, a spontaneous process has Delta S_total >= 0; equality describes the ideal reversible limit.
- **Irreversibility and the statistical arrow of time.** — The macroscopic arrow of time appears because evolution from a rare state almost always enters a vastly larger set of equilibrium microstates.
- **Entropy can decrease locally in an open system through exchange with its surroundings.** — A refrigerator lowers entropy inside its cabinet, but input work and rejected heat raise surroundings entropy by at least as much.

## Short example

- **Problem:** One mole of ideal gas freely expands inside an isolated vessel from volume V to 2V. Find the gas entropy change.
- **Answer:** The gas entropy increases by 5.76 J/K.
- **Check:** The isolated gas gained accessible volume, increasing its microstate count and entropy despite Q = 0 on the real path.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> S = k_B ln(Omega) requires an equiprobable set, while -k_B sum p_i ln p_i requires normalized discrete classical probabilities; continuous and quantum systems require a suitably refined measure and definition.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> The entropy integral uses reversible heat on an auxiliary path; inserting actual irreversible-process heat, such as zero in free expansion, gives a wrong result.

## Self-check

1. Does crystallization violate the second law because the material entropy decreases?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Expand the system to include the surroundings that receive latent heat.
> No. Crystal entropy decrease is offset by an equal or larger surroundings increase; the law applies to the complete isolated composite.

2. How does entropy change if equiprobable microstates increase from Omega to 4Omega?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Subtract k_B ln(Omega) from k_B ln(4Omega).
> Delta S = k_B ln 4 = 2 k_B ln 2.
