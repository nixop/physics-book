# 8.4. Internal Energy and the First Law

> [!abstract] In brief
> Internal energy U contains microscopic kinetic and potential contributions within the chosen system and is a state function. Heat Q and work W characterize transfers across its boundary, so a system does not contain an amount of heat or work. Their values depend on the path between states.

## Core explanation

Adopt the convention Q positive into the system and W positive when done by the system on its surroundings. Then $\Delta U = Q - W_{\mathrm{total}}$. For a simple compressible body, boundary work is the integral of $p_{\mathrm{ext}}\,dV$; it equals total work only when electrical, chemical, surface, and other work channels are absent.

## Key ideas

- **Internal energy is a state function.** — The change in U is determined only by initial and final equilibrium states, although the division of transferred energy into Q and W depends on process.
- **Heat and work transfer energy across a system boundary; they are not quantities stored in the system.** — Heat transfer is driven by temperature difference, while work is organized transfer through a generalized force and displacement; after crossing the boundary, energy belongs to U or macroscopic energy.
- **With `Q > 0` into the system and `W > 0` done by the system, the first law is `ΔU = Q − W`.** — With this convention, heat input raises the balance and positive work by the system lowers it; another convention changes the sign before W together with its definition.
- **Here `W` is the total work done by the system; `∫p_ext dV` describes only boundary pV work.** — $W_{\mathrm{total}}$ sums every work mode, whereas the pV term accounts only for motion of a mechanical boundary against external pressure.
- **When electrical, chemical, and other work channels are absent, the area under `p_ext(V)` gives the total work.** — Area under $p_{\mathrm{ext}}(V)$ has units $Pa m^3 = J$ and gives all work only if no other work coordinates are present.
- **Gas heat capacities depend on the constraints of the process.** — For example, $C_V$ describes heating at fixed volume with no pV work, while $C_p$ includes energy needed for expansion at fixed pressure.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A closed gas absorbs $Q = 2.20$ kJ and expands by 0.0100 m^3 against constant external pressure 150 kPa. No other work occurs. Find W and $\Delta U$ with W positive when done by the system.
- **Answer:** $W = +1.50$ kJ and $\Delta U = +0.70$ kJ.
- **Check:** The 2.20 kJ input splits into 1.50 kJ of work and a 0.70 kJ increase in internal energy.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The balance requires a declared system and sign convention; $W_{\mathrm{pV}} = \int p_{\mathrm{ext}} dV$ assumes a simple moving boundary and external pressure, while using system pressure along the path additionally requires quasistatic mechanical equilibrium.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Do not say that a body 'contains heat': heat denotes energy crossing a boundary because of temperature difference, while internal energy describes storage.

## Formulas

- [[../Formula Index#First Law of Thermodynamics|First Law of Thermodynamics]] — ΔU = Q − W_total; W_pV = ∫ p_ext dV

## Self-check

1. Why do Q and W depend on path while $\Delta U$ does not?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Compare two processes between identical states with different pV paths.
> U is fixed by states, but work area and required heat transfer can differ; the first law preserves the same difference Q - W.

2. A system receives 500 J as heat, and an external force does 120 J of work on it. Find $\Delta U$ under this convention.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Work on the system means $W_{\mathrm{by\,system}}=-120\,\mathrm J$.
> $\Delta U = 500 - (-120) = 620\,\mathrm{J}$.
