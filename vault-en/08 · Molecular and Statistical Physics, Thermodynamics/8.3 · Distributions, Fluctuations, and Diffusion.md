# 8.3. Distributions, Fluctuations, and Diffusion

> [!abstract] In brief
> Collisions continually redistribute energy among molecules, so equal temperature does not mean equal speed. The equilibrium Maxwell speed distribution contains many slow particles, a maximum at an intermediate speed, and a decaying high-speed tail. Heating increases characteristic speeds and broadens the distribution.

## Core explanation

Macroscopic averages are stable because particle number is large, while relative fluctuations usually fall approximately as 1/sqrt(N) for weakly correlated contributions. A random walk has zero mean displacement but a nonzero mean-square displacement; in ordinary diffusion, that quantity grows linearly with time.

## Key ideas

- **Why molecules do not all have the same speed.** — Elastic collisions conserve total energy while changing individual particle energies, so equilibrium specifies a probability distribution rather than one speed.
- **A qualitative view of the Maxwell speed distribution.** — The speed distribution depends on temperature and mass: raising T moves characteristic speeds upward, while raising mass at equal T moves them downward.
- **Mean values and fluctuations.** — A mean describes the statistical center, whereas a fluctuation is the departure of one measurement; relative variations are more visible in a small subsystem.
- **Random walks and diffusion.** — For independent steps, random directions cancel mean displacement while variances add, so rms distance grows as sqrt(N), not N.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A particle takes independent one-dimensional steps of length 1.0 micrometer to the right or left with equal probability. Find mean displacement and rms distance after 10000 steps.
- **Answer:** Mean displacement is zero, while x_rms = 100 micrometers.
- **Check:** The straight-line path length would be 10 mm; random reversal reduces typical net displacement to the sqrt(N) scale.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Normal random-walk diffusion assumes finite step variance, weak correlations, and a homogeneous medium; directed drift, trapping, long memory, or heavy-tailed steps may produce anomalous diffusion.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Zero mean displacement does not mean the particle remains near the origin; the distribution width continues to grow.

## Self-check

1. What happens to one gas's speed distribution when temperature rises?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Connect characteristic kinetic energy with k_B T.
> The maximum shifts toward higher speed, the distribution broadens, and the fraction of fast particles increases.

2. By what factor does random-walk rms displacement change if the number of independent steps increases ninefold?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> x_rms is proportional to sqrt(N).
> It increases by a factor of 3.
