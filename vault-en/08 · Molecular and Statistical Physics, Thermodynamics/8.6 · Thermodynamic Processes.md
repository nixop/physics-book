# 8.6. Thermodynamic Processes

> [!abstract] In brief
> Isochoric, isobaric, isothermal, and adiabatic processes impose different constraints: fixed V, p, T, or no heat transfer, respectively. They are not interchangeable. A quasistatic process passes through a sequence of near-equilibrium states, allowing system pressure and temperature to be defined at each step.

## Core explanation

On a pV diagram, boundary work by the system is the oriented area under a path when the pressure used for work is appropriate. Over a complete cycle U returns to its initial value, so Delta U_cycle = 0 and net heat equals net work under the chosen convention. An irreversible real path may not possess one well-defined internal pV curve.

## Key ideas

- **Isochoric, isobaric, isothermal, and adiabatic processes.** — Isothermal does not mean Q = 0, and adiabatic does not mean Delta T = 0; the constraints are related through the first law and equation of state.
- **Quasistatic change.** — Quasistatic change requires relaxation much faster than external driving; it does not by itself guarantee reversibility when friction or a finite temperature gradient is present.
- **A cycle on a pV diagram.** — A closed loop has oriented area: clockwise traversal usually corresponds to positive net pV work by the system.
- **Work, heat, and internal energy along each leg of a process.** — Each leg separately obeys Delta U = Q - W, and the sums over a cycle must give zero change for every state function.
- **Real and idealized processes.** — Idealizations make a path calculable, while real processes include finite pressure and temperature differences, viscosity, leakage, and dissipation.

## Short example

- **Problem:** One mole of monatomic ideal gas is heated quasistatically at constant pressure from 300 to 450 K. No other work occurs. Find W, Delta U, and Q using R = 8.314 J/(mol K) and W positive when done by the gas.
- **Answer:** W = 1.25 kJ, Delta U = 1.87 kJ, and Q = 3.12 kJ.
- **Check:** Q exceeds Delta U because part of the supplied energy became positive expansion work.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Equilibrium pV paths and tabulated heat capacities apply to quasistatic states of a simple compressible system; during rapid irreversible expansion, system pressure may be nonuniform and work must be computed from boundary external pressure.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> An adiabatic process need not be isothermal: with Q = 0, work usually changes internal energy and temperature.

## Formulas

- [[../Formula Index#First Law of Thermodynamics|First Law of Thermodynamics]] — ΔU = Q − W_total; W_pV = ∫ p_ext dV

## Self-check

1. Can a quasistatic process be irreversible?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Quasistatic means near equilibrium, but consider friction.
> Yes. Slow piston motion with dry friction can pass through near-equilibrium states while dissipation makes the process irreversible.

2. A gas completes a cycle and does 400 J of net work. Find the net heat received by the gas under this chapter's convention.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> For a cycle, Delta U = 0.
> From 0 = Q - W, Q = +400 J.
