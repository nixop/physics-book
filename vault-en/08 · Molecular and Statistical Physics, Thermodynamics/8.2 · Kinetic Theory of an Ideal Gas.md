# 8.2. Kinetic Theory of an Ideal Gas

> [!abstract] In brief
> In the kinetic model, ideal-gas particles are small compared with their separations and interact only through elastic collisions. Changes of normal momentum in wall collisions produce pressure. Velocity isotropy connects the mean square of one component to mean-square speed.

## Core explanation

In a classical gas, each quadratic translational degree of freedom carries mean energy k_B T/2, so mean translational kinetic energy is 3k_B T/2 per particle. Combining this with the mechanical pressure expression gives pV = Nk_B T. Internal rotational and vibrational modes require separate treatment and may be quantum mechanically frozen out.

## Key ideas

- **Pressure as a result of particle collisions.** — Pressure is the mean normal momentum flux to a wall; more particles or a larger mean-square speed increase the rate and strength of impacts.
- **For a classical ideal gas, the mean translational kinetic energy per particle is `⟨K_trans⟩ = 3k_B T/2`.** — The value 3k_B T/2 concerns only three classical translational components and does not say that all particles have the same energy.
- **The internal rotational and vibrational degrees of freedom of a polyatomic molecule are counted separately.** — Rotation and vibration add heat-capacity contributions when their energy levels are accessible at the stated temperature; simple classical degree counting does not always work.
- **The ideal-gas equation of state.** — The equation pV = nRT = Nk_B T connects equilibrium macroscopic variables but does not itself specify the velocity distribution.
- **The limits of the ideal-gas model.** — The model degrades at high density, with strong intermolecular interactions, near condensation, and in a quantum-degenerate regime.

## Short example

- **Problem:** One mole of monatomic ideal gas is at T = 300 K in a volume of 24.6 L. Find pressure and mean translational kinetic energy per particle. Use R = 8.314 J/(mol K) and k_B = 1.380649e-23 J/K.
- **Answer:** p is approximately 101 kPa, and mean translational kinetic energy is 6.21e-21 J per particle.
- **Check:** A molar volume near 24.6 L at room temperature is consistent with pressure near one atmosphere.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> An ideal gas requires equilibrium, negligible particle volume, and weak interactions outside elastic collisions; the classical energy formula additionally requires nondegenerate statistics and accessible degrees of freedom.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Do not turn a mean energy into the claim that every molecule has energy 3k_B T/2; the speed distribution remains broad.

## Self-check

1. Why do lighter molecules move faster on average than heavier ones at equal temperature?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Relate characteristic translational energy to a quantity of order m v^2.
> Mean translational energy is fixed by temperature, so characteristic mean-square speed is inversely proportional to particle mass.

2. How many moles of ideal gas occupy 0.050 m^3 at 200 kPa and 400 K?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use n = pV/(RT).
> n = 200000 x 0.050/(8.314 x 400), approximately 3.01 mol.
