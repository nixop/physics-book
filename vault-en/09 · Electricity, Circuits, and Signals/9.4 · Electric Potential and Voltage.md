# 9.4. Electric Potential and Voltage

> [!abstract] In brief
> For an electrostatic field, work between two points is path-independent, so potential energy U and potential $\varphi = U/q$ can be defined for a probe charge. The difference $V_B-V_A$ is energy change per unit positive charge and is opposite to field work per unit charge.

## Core explanation

Field points toward steepest potential decrease: $E = -\nabla(\varphi)$. Therefore E is perpendicular to an equipotential surface and displacement along it does not change U. The zero of $\varphi$ is conventional; differences are physical unless boundary conditions establish a reference.

## Key ideas

- **The potential energy of a charge.** — Potential energy belongs to the charge configuration; moving probe q changes it by $q\Delta\varphi$.
- **Electric potential.** — Potential is a source scalar, so point-charge contributions add algebraically more simply than vector fields.
- **Potential difference.** — Voltage from A to B is $\Delta V = V_{\mathrm{B}} - V_{\mathrm{A}}$; its sign follows point order, not the word voltage alone.
- **Equipotential surfaces.** — Tangential electrostatic field vanishes on an equipotential surface, or the field would do work along that surface.
- **The relationship between the electric field and the spatial variation of potential.** — The minus sign in $E = -\nabla(\varphi)$ indicates motion toward lower potential, and a steep spatial slope means strong field.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A +2.0 nC source is in vacuum. Find $\Delta V = V_{\mathrm{B}} - V_{\mathrm{A}}$ from $r_{\mathrm{A}} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ to $r_{\mathrm{B}} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ with $V(\infty)=0$ and $k = 8.99 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N m^2/C^2}$.
- **Answer:** $\Delta V = -53.9\,\mathrm{V}$; potential falls on moving away from a positive charge.
- **Check:** Potential approaches zero as r increases, so the final value must be below the initial value.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> One scalar electrostatic potential describes a curl-free field of stationary charges; a changing magnetic flux gives nonzero circulation of E, so $\varphi$ alone is insufficient.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Positive potential does not imply positive potential energy for every probe: $U=qV$ changes sign with q.

## Self-check

1. Can field be nonzero at a point where the selected potential is zero?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Field depends on gradient, while potential zero may be shifted by a constant.
> Yes. One value of V depends on reference, whereas E depends on spatial variation of V.

2. A $+3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ charge crosses $\Delta V = -20\,\mathrm{V}$. Find $\Delta U$.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use $\Delta U = q \Delta V$.
> $\Delta U = 3.0 \times 10^{-6} \times (-20) = -6.0 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{J}$.
