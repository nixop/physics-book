# 9.7. Sources, Power, and Energy Transfer

> [!abstract] In brief
> A source's electromotive force is work by non-electrostatic processes per unit charge moved inside the source. A real source has internal resistance or a more complicated impedance, so terminal voltage under load differs from emf and some power dissipates internally.

## Core explanation

In a resistive load, P = VI = I^2 R = V^2/R when V and I refer to that element. Maximum simple-load power occurs at R_load = r_internal, but then half the power is lost in the source, so this is not maximum efficiency. Energy flows through electromagnetic fields around conductors; electrons support current but are not a consumable energy supply stored in the wire.

## Key ideas

- **Electromotive force.** — Emf has units volts and describes energy per charge supplied by chemical, mechanical, or other forces, not a mechanical force in newtons.
- **The internal resistance of a source.** — For emf epsilon in series with r during discharge, V_terminal = epsilon - Ir; the drop Ir grows with current and transfers energy into internal heat.
- **Power and Joule heating.** — Joule heating results as the field gives energy to carriers and they scatter into the lattice; the usable P expression depends on known variables and whether the element is ohmic.
- **Load matching.** — Maximum-power matching is useful for signals, but energy sources usually use R_load much greater than r for high efficiency.
- **Why electrical energy is not transported by a ‘supply of electrons in the wire.’** — The Poynting vector describes field-energy flow into a load from surrounding space, while carrier drift closes the electrical circuit.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A source has emf 12 V and internal resistance 1.0 ohm. A 5.0 ohm load is connected. Find current, terminal voltage, load power, and internal loss.
- **Answer:** I = 2.0 A, V_terminal = 10 V, P_load = 20 W, and internal loss is 4.0 W.
- **Check:** The power balance closes, and terminal voltage is below emf during discharge.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> A constant emf plus one internal resistance works only over a limited current, temperature, and state-of-charge range; a real battery has dynamics, nonlinearity, power limits, and electrochemical loss.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Maximum load power is not maximum efficiency: at R_load = r, simple-circuit efficiency is only 50%.

## Self-check

1. Why does battery voltage sag when a heavy load is connected?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Include the internal drop Ir.
> A larger current produces greater internal voltage drop and loss, reducing V_terminal = epsilon - Ir.

2. A 9 V source with r = 0.50 ohm drives R = 4.0 ohm. Find current.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> The resistances are series elements in this model.
> I = 9/(4.0 + 0.50) = 2.0 A.
