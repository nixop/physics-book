# 9.8. Electric Circuits and Measuring Instruments

> [!abstract] In brief
> In series, one current passes through every element and voltages add. In parallel, branch voltage is shared and current divides. Kirchhoff's junction rule expresses charge conservation, while its loop rule expresses energy balance in a quasistatic lumped circuit.

## Core explanation

An ideal ammeter has zero resistance and is inserted in series; an ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance and is connected in parallel. A real meter changes the circuit: an ammeter adds resistance and a voltmeter draws current. A short circuit bypasses a load with low resistance and can cause dangerous current, heating, and arcing.

## Key ideas

- **Series and parallel connections.** — Equivalent series resistance is a sum, while parallel conductances add; these rules follow from common current or common branch voltage.
- **Kirchhoff's circuit laws.** — Signed currents sum to zero at a junction, and signed potential changes sum to zero around a closed loop when inductive effects are negligible.
- **Ammeters, voltmeters, and the effect of a measuring instrument on a circuit.** — Input resistance and bandwidth set loading and dynamic error, so a reading belongs to the modified circuit rather than an ideally undisturbed object.
- **Short circuits and electrical safety.** — A fuse or breaker limits overcurrent consequences but does not make contact safe; hazard also depends on voltage, body path, duration, and contact conditions.

## Short example

- **Problem:** Two $10\,\mathrm{k}\Omega$ resistors divide 5.0 V. Voltage across the lower resistor is measured by a voltmeter with $10\,\mathrm{k}\Omega$ input resistance. Find its reading.
- **Answer:** The meter reads about 1.67 V and substantially changes the measured circuit.
- **Check:** The effective lower branch decreased, so its fraction of total voltage must fall below one half.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Lumped-circuit laws require dimensions much smaller than signal wavelength and negligible distributed fields; at high frequency, wires have inductance, capacitance, propagation delay, and radiation.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Never connect an ammeter directly in parallel with a source: its low resistance creates a near short circuit.

## Self-check

1. Why should a good voltmeter have high input resistance?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Estimate current it draws from the measured node.
> High resistance reduces additional current and therefore reduces disturbance of the original circuit voltage.

2. Resistors $6\,\Omega$ and $3\,\Omega$ are parallel. Find equivalent resistance.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Add the conductances: $1/R_{\mathrm{eq}}=1/6+1/3$.
> $1/R_{\mathrm{eq}}=1/2$, so $R_{\mathrm{eq}}=2\,\Omega$.
