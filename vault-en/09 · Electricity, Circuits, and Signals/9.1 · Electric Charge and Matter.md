# 9.1. Electric Charge and Matter

> [!abstract] In brief
> Electric charge sets the sign and strength of electromagnetic interaction. Like charges repel and unlike charges attract, but force direction should follow from the vector law rather than a separate mnemonic. Total charge is conserved in a closed system; an object's charge changes by transfer across its boundary.

## Core explanation

In ordinary matter, charge occurs in integer multiples of elementary charge within the particle description. Mobile carriers redistribute in a conductor, while bound positive and negative charges shift slightly in a dielectric and create polarization. Grounding connects an object to a large charge reservoir and fixes its potential only within the Earth-as-conductor model.

## Key ideas

- **Positive and negative charge.** — The labels positive and negative are conventional, but relative sign is measurable because it determines force direction and contribution to total charge.
- **Conservation and quantization of charge.** — Local charge continuity connects change inside a volume to current through its surface; quantization gives Q = n e for an isolated collection of ordinary carriers.
- **Conductors, dielectrics, and polarization.** — A conductor has carriers able to move macroscopically, whereas a dielectric responds mainly through small displacement of bound positive and negative charge.
- **Electrostatic induction and grounding.** — A charged object can separate charges in a neutral conductor without contact; grounding lets carriers enter or leave, and disconnection order determines residual charge.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A small body acquires charge Q = -3.204e-16 C. How many excess electrons did it receive? Use e = 1.602e-19 C.
- **Answer:** The body received 2000 excess electrons.
- **Check:** N is dimensionless and integral; total electron charge -Ne reproduces Q.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Charge conservation is fundamental, but a simple picture of point electrons and linear polarization does not describe breakdown, electrochemistry, strong-field ionization, or frequency-dependent material response.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> An electrically neutral object is not devoid of charges: its total positive and negative charges cancel but can still polarize.

## Self-check

1. Why can a charged rod attract a neutral dielectric fragment?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Consider a small displacement of bound charges and field nonuniformity.
> The field polarizes the dielectric; nearer opposite bound charge feels a larger force than farther like charge, producing net attraction.

2. What is the charge after 5.0e6 electrons are removed from a body?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Removing negative charge leaves positive charge; Q = Ne.
> Q = +5.0e6 x 1.602e-19 C = +8.01e-13 C.
