# 2.5. Free Fall and Projectile Motion

> [!abstract] In brief
> Near Earth's surface over modest heights, gravity can be treated as uniform: acceleration points vertically downward and is approximately g. Without air drag, horizontal velocity remains constant while vertical velocity evolves independently under constant acceleration.

## Core explanation

A common time parameter joins the components into a parabolic trajectory. Range, height, and flight time follow from initial speed, angle, and height difference; mass cancels because gravitational force and inertia contain the same mass.

## Key ideas

- **Acceleration due to gravity.** — In a local frame with +y upward, aᵧ = −g. The value of g varies slightly with latitude and altitude, but 9.81 m/s² is normally sufficient for near-surface exercises.
- **In a uniform field without drag, horizontal and vertical motion share only the same time coordinate.** — The equations x = x₀ + v₀ₓt and y = y₀ + v₀ᵧt − gt²/2 share t, but the horizontal equation contains no vertical quantity. That is component independence in this model.
- **Horizontal and angled projectile motion.** — A horizontal launch has v₀ᵧ = 0, while an angled launch has v₀ₓ = v₀cos θ and v₀ᵧ = v₀sin θ. After decomposition, the same component equations apply.
- **Range, maximum height, and time of flight.** — For equal launch and landing heights, flight time is 2v₀sin θ/g, maximum height above launch is v₀²sin²θ/(2g), and range is v₀²sin 2θ/g.
- **Air drag couples the components of motion and changes the trajectory.** — Drag depends on velocity relative to air and usually couples the components. It shortens range, destroys ascent-descent symmetry, and makes the trajectory nonparabolic.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A projectile is launched at 20 m/s and 30° above horizontal. Find flight time, range, and maximum height for g = 9.81 m/s² with no air drag.
- **Answer:** Flight time is about 2.04 s, range 35.3 m, and maximum height above launch 5.10 m.
- **Check:** Ascent lasts v₀ᵧ/g ≈ 1.02 s, exactly half the full time in this symmetric model.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> A parabolic path requires uniform g, no drag, locally flat geometry, and constant mass; long, fast, or aerodynamically important flights need a numerical model including curvature, Earth rotation, and drag.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> The range equation R = v₀²sin 2θ/g assumes equal launch and landing heights; it cannot be used for a cliff launch without solving the vertical equation.

## Self-check

1. Two bodies of different mass are released together from one height in vacuum. Which lands first?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Apply the second law with gravitational force mg.
> They land together under equal initial conditions: ma = mg gives a = g independently of mass.

2. A body is launched horizontally at 10 m/s from a height of 20 m. Estimate flight time and range for g = 9.81 m/s².

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> First solve 0 = 20 − gt²/2, then use x = v₀ₓt.
> t = √(40/9.81) ≈ 2.02 s and R ≈ 10 × 2.02 = 20.2 m without air drag.
