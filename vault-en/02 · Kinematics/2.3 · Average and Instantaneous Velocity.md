# 2.3. Average and Instantaneous Velocity

> [!abstract] In brief
> Average speed is total distance divided by duration and carries no direction. Average velocity uses displacement, so it can be zero even after a large distance has been traveled.

## Core explanation

Instantaneous velocity is the limit over ever shorter intervals, the time derivative of position. It is tangent to the trajectory and points along the motion, while its magnitude is instantaneous speed.

## Key ideas

- **Average speed.** — The quantity s/Δt averages the rate at which distance accumulates and is nonnegative. It cannot recover direction or final position.
- **Average velocity.** — The vector Δr/Δt joins the endpoints of the selected interval. Its magnitude cannot exceed average speed because |Δr| ≤ s.
- **Instantaneous velocity as a limit and as the slope of a graph.** — The limit of Δr/Δt as Δt → 0 is dr/dt and geometrically gives the tangent slope of a position graph. It describes local motion, not the preceding route.
- **The direction of velocity along a curved trajectory.** — On a smooth curve, velocity is tangent to the path. Its direction changes in a turn even when speed remains constant.

## Short example

- **Problem:** An object moves 100 m east and then 60 m west in a total time of 80 s. Find average speed and average velocity.
- **Answer:** Average speed is 2.0 m/s and average velocity is 0.50 m/s east.
- **Check:** Average velocity magnitude is smaller than average speed because part of the route was retraced.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Instantaneous velocity is defined where the trajectory is differentiable; an ideal instantaneous corner implies unbounded acceleration and requires a more detailed transition model.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Segment speeds generally cannot be combined by a simple arithmetic mean: their weights are segment times, and equal distances usually take unequal times.

## Self-check

1. Can instantaneous velocity be nonzero when average velocity over a full interval is zero?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Imagine completing a full lap and returning to the start.
> Yes. Displacement and average velocity over a full lap are zero, while instantaneous velocity is nonzero and tangent to the circle at ordinary points of the motion.

2. Position is x(t) = 2t² + 1 m. Find instantaneous velocity at t = 3 s.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Differentiate x with respect to t.
> v(t) = 4t m/s, so v(3 s) = 12 m/s along +x.
