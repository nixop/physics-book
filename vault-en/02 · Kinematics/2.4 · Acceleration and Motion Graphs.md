# 2.4. Acceleration and Motion Graphs

> [!abstract] In brief
> Acceleration is the derivative of vector velocity, so it responds to a change in either magnitude or direction. Uniform circular motion has nonzero inward acceleration even though speed is constant.

## Core explanation

The graphs x(t), v(t), and a(t) are connected by derivatives and integrals. The sign of acceleration alone does not mean speeding up: speed grows when velocity and acceleration align and falls when they oppose.

## Key ideas

- **Acceleration as a change in the velocity vector.** — Average acceleration is Δv/Δt and instantaneous acceleration is dv/dt. Because vectors are subtracted, equal speed magnitudes in different directions still produce a nonzero change.
- **Speeding up, slowing down, and turning.** — In one dimension a body speeds up when va > 0 and slows when va < 0. In several dimensions a perpendicular change can bend the path without changing speed.
- **The relationship among position, velocity, and acceleration graphs.** — The slope of x(t) is v and the slope of v(t) is a; signed area under v(t) is displacement and under a(t) is velocity change. An extremum of x often has v = 0 but need not imply rest over a finite interval.
- **Motion with constant acceleration.** — With constant acceleration, velocity is linear in time and position is quadratic. The equations require one inertial frame and consistent initial conditions.
- **Motion with variable acceleration and numerical integration.** — For variable acceleration, motion is obtained by integration or time stepping. The step must be short compared with the acceleration's change timescale and convergence must be checked.

## Short example

- **Problem:** Along a chosen axis, v(t) = 4 − 2t m/s from 0 to 3 s. Find acceleration, displacement, and distance traveled.
- **Answer:** Acceleration is −2 m/s², displacement is +3 m, and distance is 5 m; the body first slows and then speeds up along −x.
- **Check:** Final velocity −2 m/s confirms that the last segment subtracts from displacement but adds to distance.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Constant-acceleration equations fail when acceleration changes appreciably with time, position, or velocity; a piecewise-constant approximation needs an error check at step boundaries.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down: if velocity is also negative, their alignment increases speed.

## Self-check

1. A car travels around a circle at a constant 15 m/s. Does it accelerate?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Velocity is a vector, not only its magnitude.
> Yes. Velocity direction changes continuously, so the car has inward normal acceleration even though speed is constant.

2. An object moves in a circle of radius 20 m at 10 m/s. Find normal acceleration.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use aₙ = v²/R.
> aₙ = (10 m/s)² / 20 m = 5.0 m/s² toward the center.
