# 15. Astrophysics and Cosmology

> [!info] Section
> 7 cards · about 26 planned pages · level: advanced

## Cards

- [[15.1 · How We Measure the Universe|15.1. How We Measure the Universe]] — Astronomical measurement is usually an inverse problem: a telescope records angles, times, flux, and spectra, while a physical model connects them to source properties.
- [[15.2 · A Star's Equilibrium and Energy|15.2. A Star's Equilibrium and Energy]] — Gravity tends to compress a star, heating matter and increasing gas and radiation pressure.
- [[15.3 · Stellar Evolution and the Origin of the Elements|15.3. Stellar Evolution and the Origin of the Elements]] — The Hertzsprung-Russell diagram compares luminosity with effective temperature or spectral class.
- [[15.4 · Compact Objects|15.4. Compact Objects]] — A white dwarf is supported mainly by electron degeneracy pressure, while a neutron star uses degeneracy and short-range nuclear interactions at much greater density.
- [[15.5 · General Relativity as Observable Physics|15.5. General Relativity as Observable Physics]] — Curved spacetime changes paths of light and freely moving bodies: lenses produce shifts, arcs, and multiple images, while orbits acquire corrections to Newtonian precession.
- [[15.6 · Galaxies, Dark Matter, and Large-Scale Structure|15.6. Galaxies, Dark Matter, and Large-Scale Structure]] — Stars, gas, dust, and central compact objects form a galaxy's visible component, but their motions probe the total potential.
- [[15.7 · The Expanding Universe|15.7. The Expanding Universe]] — In a homogeneous cosmological model, separation between unbound comoving objects grows with scale factor a(t).
