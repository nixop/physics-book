# 3.2. Inertia and Newton's First Law

> [!abstract] In brief
> The first law rejects the idea that force maintains velocity. If external forces cancel, a body stays at rest or moves uniformly in a straight line; force is required to change the velocity vector.

## Core explanation

The law also defines the class of inertial frames. Any frame moving uniformly without rotation relative to an inertial frame is also inertial, and internal mechanical experiments cannot identify absolute uniform rest.

## Key ideas

- **Why motion does not require a continuous force.** — After a push ends, an ideal body need not stop. Everyday objects slow because of friction and drag, not because a sustaining force is absent.
- **Inertial frames of reference.** — An inertial frame is one in which a free body has constant velocity. Acceleration or rotation of a frame appears as coordinated apparent deviations of many free bodies.
- **Galilean relativity.** — Galilean transformations preserve acceleration and the form of Newtonian mechanical laws between uniformly moving inertial frames. No internal mechanical experiment determines their absolute velocity.
- **Mass as a measure of inertia.** — Under the same resultant force, a body with greater mass has less acceleration. This operationally expresses inertia, although mass also enters other physical theories.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A puck is given velocity 6 m/s along +x, after which net external force is negligible. Where is it after 8 s if x(0) = 2 m?
- **Answer:** After 8 s the puck is at x = 50 m if resistance is genuinely negligible.
- **Check:** Position increases by the same 6 m during every second, which is constant velocity.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The first law in this form concerns inertial frames of classical mechanics; Earth is only approximately inertial in problems where effects of its rotation and orbital acceleration are negligible.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Zero resultant does not mean zero velocity; it means zero acceleration and therefore a constant velocity vector.

## Self-check

1. A passenger in a closed railcar moving uniformly in a straight line throws a ball vertically. Can the experiment determine the car's speed relative to Earth?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Compare mechanical laws in two inertial frames.
> No. Neglecting vibration and air effects, the ball returns to the passenger just as in a stationary car; a mechanical experiment does not identify uniform speed relative to Earth.

2. A body moves at (3, −4) m/s under zero resultant force for 5 s. Find its displacement.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> With a = 0, multiply each velocity component by time.
> Δr = (15, −20) m and displacement magnitude is 25 m.
