# 3.3. Newton's Second Law

> [!abstract] In brief
> Newton's second law connects the cause of motion change to the momentum derivative: ΣFext = dp/dt for a material system of fixed composition. It is a vector law, so one equation becomes independent component equations along chosen axes.

## Core explanation

When mass is constant, dp/dt = m dv/dt and ΣF = ma follows. Predicting a trajectory also requires models for the individual forces and initial conditions for position and velocity.

## Key ideas

- **The net external force.** — All forces exerted by objects outside the selected system are added. One large force need not cause acceleration if other forces provide an opposite sum.
- **For a material system of fixed composition, the net external force equals the time derivative of its momentum.** — Momentum of a material system p = Σmᵢvᵢ changes through external force. Fixed composition excludes particles crossing the boundary and carrying an additional momentum flux.
- **At constant mass, Newton's second law becomes `ΣF = ma`.** — At constant inertial mass, acceleration is parallel to the resultant and inversely proportional to mass. The equation applies to the force sum, not separately to every force.
- **Vector notation and component equations.** — Component equations ΣFₓ = maₓ and ΣFᵧ = maᵧ incorporate geometrical constraints. Component signs follow chosen axis directions, not force names.
- **Initial conditions and numerical prediction of motion.** — A second-order differential equation needs initial r(t₀) and v(t₀). Without them, the same force law permits a family of trajectories.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A 60 kg load is lifted by a cable with acceleration 1.2 m/s². Find tension for g = 9.81 m/s², choosing +y upward.
- **Answer:** Cable tension is about 661 N; it exceeds mg because the resultant is upward.
- **Check:** As a → 0, the expression returns T = mg for rest or uniform motion.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The form ΣF = dp/dt requires an inertial frame and a material system of fixed composition; a rocket or jet needs a momentum-flux balance, and at relativistic speeds p is not mv.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> The term ma is not an extra force on a free-body diagram; it is the result of summing real external forces in an inertial frame.

## Self-check

1. Several nonzero forces act on a body but their sum is zero. What follows about motion?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> The second law determines acceleration, not velocity directly.
> Acceleration is zero; depending on its initial state, the body rests or moves with a constant velocity vector.

2. A 4 kg mass experiences forces (10, 0) N and (−2, 6) N. Find acceleration.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Add force components and divide by mass.
> ΣF = (8, 6) N, so a = (2.0, 1.5) m/s² and acceleration magnitude is 2.5 m/s².
