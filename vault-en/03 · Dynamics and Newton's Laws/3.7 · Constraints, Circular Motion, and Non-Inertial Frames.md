# 3.7. Constraints, Circular Motion, and Non-Inertial Frames

> [!abstract] In brief
> A constraint limits allowed positions and velocities, while support or tension forces provide the required acceleration. Centripetal force is not a new interaction but a name for the inward sum of components of real forces.

## Core explanation

In a rotating frame, free bodies appear to deflect. To retain the second-law form in those coordinates, centrifugal and Coriolis inertial forces are introduced, together with an Euler force when angular velocity changes.

## Key ideas

- **Geometric constraints and constrained motion.** — A geometrical condition such as fixed rod length relates coordinates of system parts. Constraint reaction is found by solving that condition together with equations of motion.
- **Which real force plays the role of the centripetal force.** — On a turn, radial force may be supplied by friction, tension, gravity, or their sum. Adding a separate centripetal-force arrow on top of these forces would double-count the same resultant.
- **A rotating frame of reference.** — Axes of a rotating frame change orientation relative to an inertial frame. Even a body with constant inertial velocity has an apparent acceleration in rotating coordinates.
- **The centrifugal effect and the Coriolis force.** — The centrifugal term points away from the rotation axis, while the Coriolis term −2mΩ×vrel is perpendicular to relative velocity. They are features of a non-inertial description, not new interactions.
- **The Foucault pendulum and atmospheric vortices.** — A Foucault pendulum's swing plane remains approximately fixed in inertial space while Earth turns beneath it. The Coriolis effect also influences large-scale atmospheric flow but does not supply its energy.

## Short example

- **Problem:** A car takes a flat curve of radius 50 m at 10 m/s. What minimum static-friction coefficient is required for g = 9.81 m/s²?
- **Answer:** The static-friction coefficient must be at least about 0.204; practical margins must allow for roughness and changing speed.
- **Check:** Mass cancels, so the ideal-model speed limit does not depend on vehicle mass.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> Equations for a uniformly rotating frame assume a known Ω vector; changing Ω requires an Euler term, while Earth's rotation can often be neglected in small laboratory problems only after estimating the effect.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> A centrifugal force must not be added to a diagram in an inertial frame alongside real forces; it is introduced only in equations using rotating coordinates.

## Formulas

- [[../Formula Index#Newton's Second Law|Newton's Second Law]] — ΣF = dp/dt; for constant mass, ΣF = ma

## Self-check

1. A satellite follows a circular orbit. Which force supplies centripetal force?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Look for a real interaction directed toward the orbit center.
> The planet's gravitational force. There is no separate additional centripetal force.

2. A 0.50 kg ball moves in a circle of radius 2.0 m at 4.0 m/s. Find the required radial resultant.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Use F_r = mv²/R.
> F_r = 0.50×16/2.0 = 4.0 N toward the center. Which particular force supplies it depends on the apparatus.
