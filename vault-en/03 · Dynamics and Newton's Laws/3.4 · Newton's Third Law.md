# 3.4. Newton's Third Law

> [!abstract] In brief
> If body A acts on B, B simultaneously acts on A with an equal and opposite force. The forces belong to one interaction, have the same physical nature, and act on different bodies.

## Core explanation

A third-law pair does not establish equilibrium of one body because its arrows cannot be added in one equation of motion. When both bodies form one system, the pair becomes internal and cancels from the total momentum balance.

## Key ideas

- **Interactions occurring in pairs.** — Pairing means F_A→B = −F_B→A in a Newtonian interaction model. A change in one force entails a simultaneous change in its partner rather than a delayed response.
- **Why action and reaction act on different bodies.** — Earth's force on an apple acts on the apple, while the apple's force on Earth acts on Earth. Equal magnitudes are compatible with vastly different accelerations because the masses differ.
- **Walking, swimming, recoil, and thrust.** — While walking, a foot pushes Earth backward and Earth pushes the person forward through static friction. A swimmer and a rocket gain momentum by giving opposite momentum to water or exhaust.
- **Resolving common paradoxes.** — A horse accelerates the horse-cart system through external friction from Earth; the internal pair between horse and cart does not forbid acceleration of the whole system. Likewise, Earth moves toward a falling body but with tiny acceleration.

## Short example

- **Problem:** Skaters of mass 50 and 75 kg start at rest and push apart. The first acquires +3.0 m/s. Find the second velocity, neglecting external horizontal impulse.
- **Answer:** The second skater moves at 2.0 m/s in the opposite direction.
- **Check:** Momenta +150 and −150 kg·m/s cancel although velocities differ.

> [!warning] Limits of the model
> The instantaneous paired-force form is sufficient in Newtonian mechanics; electromagnetic systems with finite propagation speed may require field momentum for a complete momentum balance.

> [!danger] Common mistake
> Gravity and the normal force on a resting book may be equal, but they are not a third-law pair: both act on the book and arise from different interactions.

## Self-check

1. What is the third-law partner of Earth's gravitational force on a satellite?

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Exchange the source and receiver of the interaction.
> It is the satellite's gravitational force on Earth, equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and applied to Earth.

2. A 3 kg body initially at rest ejects a 1 kg part backward at −6 m/s relative to Earth. Find the velocity of the remaining 2 kg when external impulse is zero.

> [!tip]- Answer and guide
> Total momentum before and after is zero.
> 1×(−6) + 2v = 0, so the remaining part moves at +3 m/s.
